# OpenProbe blueprint

Version 0.1 - design proposal, not a validated hiring assessment.

## Layer 1: Application check

Inputs are a resume and job description. AI structures the claims and identifies
inconsistencies worth reviewing. Each flag needs an exact supporting excerpt and
a clear reason. Missing evidence remains unknown rather than automatically poor.

Planted JD inconsistencies are an experimental idea. Repeating an impossible
claim may provide a reason to ask a question. Avoiding it does not verify
experience or establish whether the applicant used AI. Such checks must be
evaluated for false positives and candidate understanding before hiring use.

## Layer 2: Quick role quiz

- Ten short, timed questions with one-tap answers.
- Standard question banks for each role; no bespoke essay per applicant.
- Recognise technologies, their purposes and sensible combinations.
- Capture a private initial answer before showing any group suggestions.
- For selected questions, show disclosed simulated suggestions that may favour
  a correct or incorrect answer. Allow the candidate to keep or change theirs.
- Observe initial and final accuracy, answer changes, timings and session events.
- Provide accessible alternatives and timing adjustments where needed.

Timing cannot guarantee the absence of external assistance. An interruption is
an observation to review, not a cheating verdict. Group-influence behaviour is
experimental and should not be labelled as verified conviction or personality.

Optional pattern-recognition tasks receive a separate result, not an IQ score.

## Layer 3: Workplace simulation

### Knowledge transfer

Provide confirmed facts and a short chat with a disclosed simulated colleague.
Assess accuracy, audience-appropriate language, clarification and understanding
checks. The objective is transferring knowledge, not solving an undisclosed
technical puzzle or writing a long essay.

### AI delegation and work planning

Provide role-specific task cards, a goal, a deadline and the available tools and
permissions. The candidate assigns work to themselves, AI, or AI with review.
They arrange dependencies and identify work that can happen in parallel.
Assess appropriate delegation, sequencing, essential checks and ownership.
Allow multiple sound plans. A short follow-up may clarify a consequential choice.

## Scoring and feedback

Report distinct dimensions with supporting answers, excerpts and board actions.
Keep role context, rubric version and assessment date. Record human corrections.
The recruiter reviews the evidence and remains responsible for hiring decisions.
Candidates receive useful feedback. Retention and reuse for other roles require
clear candidate choices.

## First implementation

Prototype one data role. Draft scenarios and anchored scoring examples, then
compare independent human ratings with AI ratings. Evaluate completion burden,
accessibility, disagreements and whether each activity adds useful evidence.

## Unresolved choices

- The time budget and appropriate adjustments for each activity.
- Whether group suggestions add useful evidence beyond initial accuracy.
- Whether planted inconsistencies create useful signals or unnecessary confusion.
- Whether optional pattern tasks are relevant to the pilot role.
- The application stack and model provider.

No automatic rejection, AI-use detection or expertise-verification claim is
established by this blueprint.
